# Evals — continuous-improvement-skills:structured-ideation

## 1. Positive trigger (should load the skill)
> "Our recon team's improvement meetings keep landing on the same three ideas, and the manager's
> suggestion always wins. Help me run a proper ideation session — brainwriting instead of people
> shouting over each other, something to restart us when we run out of ideas, and a way to
> converge on criteria instead of applause."

Expected: skill loads; frames a single "How might we…" question and announces the hard
diverge/converge split; runs 6-3-5 brainwriting (silent, simultaneous, rotating sheets) as the
default rather than open discussion; sets a quantity target with judgment deferred; injects
SCAMPER prompts and creative constraints when output thins, offering the LLM as fatigue-proof
partner and wild-card analogy generator; pools, deduplicates, shuffles, and strips authorship
before any evaluation; converges with a dot-vote used only as a pre-filter, then an
effort/impact placement or a weighted criteria matrix with criteria named before scoring; ends
with a disposition (owner + next step or parking lot) for every survivor.

## 2. Near-miss (should NOT load this skill)
> "This reconciliation break has four plausible causes and the team is split. Help me work out
> which explanation actually fits the evidence we have."

Expected: this is weighing rival explanations against evidence —
`decision-science-skills:competing-hypotheses-analysis` owns it (hypothesis matrix, diagnostic
evidence, judgment by disconfirmation). Structured ideation generates options, not verdicts
about evidence. If structured-ideation loads here, its description has drifted toward bare
hypothesis-generation territory — tighten the Not-for line and triggers.

## 2b. Near-miss (adjacent-stage guard)
> "We want to run a two-day kaizen event on our invoice intake process — help me charter it,
> pick who should be in the room, and plan the agenda through follow-up."

Expected: end-to-end event planning belongs to
`continuous-improvement-skills:kaizen-and-codesign` (charter, gemba, PDCA, standard-work
capture, follow-up). That event may *call* structured-ideation for its idea-generation
segments, but the event itself is not this skill's job. If structured-ideation loads as the
primary skill here, the boundary line in "When to use" needs sharpening.

## 3. Quality rubric
A good response:
- **Does the task:** produces a runnable session plan with a framed question, a hard
  diverge/converge boundary, brainwriting mechanics (silent, simultaneous, rotation), a
  quantity target, at least one re-energizer (SCAMPER pass, constraint injection, or LLM
  wild-card round), an anonymized pooled list, staged convergence (dots as pre-filter only,
  then explicit criteria), and dispositions with owners.
- **Teaches:** explains production blocking (one speaker at a time — ideas die in the wait
  queue) and evaluation apprehension, attributed to Diehl & Stroebe at author level; why
  nominal groups outperform interacting groups and why silent simultaneous writing fixes both
  causes; why judgment kills generation, so the phases must be separated; why the LLM changes
  the economics (anonymous, tireless, status-blind).
- **Stays honest:** holds Osborn's rules to their evidence (the rules alone don't fix blocking
  — silence does); admits the LLM's regression-to-the-plausible-middle unless seeded; doesn't
  invent effect sizes or study statistics; keeps dot-voting as a pre-filter and says why it
  can't decide.
