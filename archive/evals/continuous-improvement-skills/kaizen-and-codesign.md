# Evals — continuous-improvement-skills:kaizen-and-codesign

## 1. Positive trigger (should load the skill)
> "I want to run a two-day kaizen event to improve our team's document-review handoff, and I
> want the people who produce the drafts and the reviewer who receives them in the room
> designing it. How do I plan and facilitate it?"

Expected: skill loads; charters a narrow, measurable, time-boxed event with a baseline and a
sponsor; puts the actual operators, the downstream customer, and an on-the-spot
decision-maker in the room; starts with a gemba walk (low-stakes elicitation, not
interrogation); runs the ideation segments as structured brainwriting → affinity → dot-vote;
runs rapid PDCA on real work the same day with a visible scoreboard; closes with
operator-written standard work, owned open items, a 30/60-day check, and a short after-action
review of the event; applies respect-for-people throughout.

## 2. Near-miss (should NOT load this skill)
> "Before the workshop, can you help me map the whole end-to-end process so we know where the
> biggest waste is?"

Expected: this is value-stream mapping that *scopes* a future event, handled by
`continuous-improvement-skills:value-stream-mapping`. Facilitating the event itself is this
skill. If kaizen-and-codesign loads instead, tighten the cross-links.

## 2b. Near-miss (ideation-technique guard)
> "Our team brainstorms and the same two people dominate every time — set up a brainwriting
> session with dot-voting so we get everyone's ideas on our naming problem."

Expected: a standalone ideation session is `continuous-improvement-skills:structured-ideation`
(kaizen events *call* it for their idea-generation segments). No process, no gemba, no
PDCA — just diverge/converge — so kaizen-and-codesign should not load as primary.

## 3. Quality rubric
A good response:
- **Does the task:** charters a tight, measurable, time-boxed event with a pre-measured
  baseline; convenes doers + downstream customer + decision-maker; goes to the gemba before
  designing; facilitates for equal voice (brainwriting before discussion, speak-last
  leaders); runs rapid, reversible PDCA loops on live work scored against the charter
  metric; and closes with operator-written standard work, a scheduled 30/60-day check, and
  an event debrief.
- **Teaches:** explains *why* those closest to the work design the most durable
  improvements, why ownership makes change stick (people support what they help create),
  and why the facilitator owns process but never content.
- **Grounded and honest:** gets the canon right — kaizen is Imai's daily,
  everyone-participates improvement and the multi-day event/blitz is a Western adaptation
  whose success is measured by whether the habit takes root; co-design descends from the
  participatory-design tradition (design authority, not a facilitation trick);
  respect-for-people is a Toyota Way pillar. Invents no statistics; example numbers labeled
  illustrative; frames problems as process problems and credits contributors.
- **Routes at the seams:** scoping the stream → value-stream-mapping; standalone ideation →
  structured-ideation; capturing the result → standard-work; carrying the story for buy-in →
  a3-thinking; tacit-knowledge interviews → disarming-elicitation; ordinary decision
  meetings → meeting-design; event debrief → after-action-review.
