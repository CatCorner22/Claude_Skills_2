# Evals — collaboration-skills:executive-briefing

## 1. Positive trigger (should load the skill)
> "I've got a 20-page analysis showing our vendor contract renewal comes in 18% higher,
> and I get five minutes with the COO on Thursday. I need a one-pager that gets an
> actual decision — renew, renegotiate, or go to RFP. Right now my draft walks through
> the methodology first and the recommendation is on page 3."

Expected: skill loads; calibrates the ask first (this is a *decide* or *approve* ask —
named, with a by-when); writes the BLUF (recommendation + ask in 1–2 sentences); frames
with SCQA; re-leads the buried-conclusion draft rather than rewriting the analysis;
builds the memo to its anatomy — options with costs including do-nothing and the price
of delay, recommendation with its reason, what happens if nothing is decided by
Thursday; runs the completed-staff-work test (could the COO just sign?); maps each memo
line to the section of the 20-pager it summarizes; offers the adams-smart-brevity
register pass and notes the memo doubles as a meeting pre-read.

## 2. Near-miss (register/prose guard)
> "Can you tighten up this email to my team? It rambles — I want it clearer and shorter,
> more scannable."

Expected: `writing-skills:adams-smart-brevity` owns sentence-level clarity, brevity, and
scanning structure — general editing with no decision-maker, no options, no ask.
executive-briefing owns the decision-document architecture, not prose mechanics. If
executive-briefing loads on a tighten-this-email ask, the boundary is failing.

## 2b. Near-miss (urgent-escalation guard)
> "A payment looks wrong and it releases in twenty minutes — help me structure what I
> say when I call the treasurer right now."

Expected: `safety-and-reliability-skills:sbar-structured-communication` owns spoken,
time-critical escalation (Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation with a
deadline). executive-briefing is the written, considered decision document — its own
body says minutes-scale deadlines are SBAR territory. If it loads here, tighten the
seam.

## 3. Quality rubric
- **Does**: names the ask (inform / decide / approve) with a by-when before drafting;
  BLUF carries recommendation + ask in 1–2 sentences; SCQA in 2–4 sentences with a real
  complication (no invented urgency); pyramid grouping checked (no overlaps, no obvious
  gaps, each line summarizes what's below); memo anatomy complete — costed options
  including do-nothing, recommendation with reason, consequence of silence with a date;
  completed-staff-work checklist run and failures named; routes slides to
  assertion-evidence-deck, rooms to meeting-design.
- **Teaches**: why answer-first converts reading into verification (the reader descends
  the pyramid only as far as trust requires); why BLUF alone is a hot take and SCQA +
  completed staff work are its governors; why an unnamed ask defaults to "inform" and
  dies; why do-nothing must be a costed option (delay is a choice, not a neutral state).
- **Stays honest**: dates the doctrine (DA Pam 600-67, 1986), never the acronym's
  regulation entry — the dossier marks that dating weak; completed staff work carries
  the dual attribution (Lerch 1942 / Smith 1943, original source unclear); Minto
  credited by name with the 1996 edition cited, never "McKinsey's method"; example
  figures labeled illustrative — no invented reading-time or attention statistics; the
  human owns the recommendation and sends the ask.
