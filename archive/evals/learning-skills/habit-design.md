# Evals — learning-skills:habit-design

## 1. Positive trigger (should load the skill)
> "I keep forgetting to write the daily position notes before the 9:30 call — I only do it
> when I have spare energy, which is never. Make it automatic."

Expected: skill loads and designs the loop rather than exhorting. It confirms this is the
one habit under construction; shrinks the routine below the failure threshold (e.g., "open
the worksheet and write one line" — the full note-set becomes the later scale target); picks
a cue at the edge of an existing stable anchor (e.g., "when I sit down with the first
coffee"); writes the implementation intention verbatim and has the user say it back, plus a
coping form for the known obstacle; runs a friction audit (worksheet pre-staged the night
before; the competing path one step longer); attaches an immediate closure signal; sets up
presence tracking with an explicit no-day-zero rule and a two-consecutive-misses redesign
trigger; and schedules a renegotiation date at design time.

## 2. Near-miss (should NOT load this skill)
> "Everyone on the team does the month-end reconciliation differently and quality depends on
> who's on shift. Standardize it so we all do it the same way."

Expected: `continuous-improvement-skills:standard-work` owns team/process standardization —
sequence, key points, and reasons in an SOP. The duality (a habit is personal standard work)
does not make a team SOP a habit-design job; if this skill loads on a team-process request,
its description is over-triggering.

## 2b. Near-miss (closer — should NOT load this skill)
> "The bar-exam grind is crushing me. I know the study plan is right — I just want to quit
> every afternoon. Hold up the mirror."

Expected: `coding-agent-skills:stay-hard-accountability` owns drive and effort on a hard
chosen goal ("hold up the mirror" is its explicit trigger). Habit design automates routines
so drive is spent elsewhere; it does not supply the drive itself.

## 3. Quality rubric
A good response:
- **Does the task:** designs exactly one habit as cue → routine → reward; sizes the starting
  routine below the failure threshold with the ambition recorded as a later scale target;
  binds the cue to a specific, already-automatic event (rejecting vague cues like "in the
  morning"); produces a verbatim implementation intention plus a coping "if <obstacle>, then
  <response>" and a pre-written resumption plan for the first miss; performs a concrete
  friction audit (good path one step shorter, competing path one step longer); attaches an
  immediate reward or closure signal; sets up presence tracking where the tiny version
  counts, a miss earns one diagnostic question, and two consecutive misses trigger redesign;
  gates scaling on weeks of stability; and books a renegotiation date where keep / scale /
  shrink / re-cue / retire are all legitimate outcomes.
- **Teaches:** explains why willpower is the wrong tool for repeated behavior (decisions are
  paid per-use and fail on depleted days; automaticity is the goal); context dependence
  (habits live in environments — change the environment first, and a context change is a
  re-design task, not a relapse); why implementation intentions work (initiation delegated
  to the cue, decided once in advance — Gollwitzer); and why streak-guilt backfires (the
  destroyed-asset cliff causes the post-miss collapse; presence keeps the data without the
  moralizing).
- **Stays honest:** labels Fogg and Clear as practitioner syntheses atop the research base
  rather than research authority; treats the headline Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006) figure
  (d ≈ 0.65, 94 tests) as an *upper* bound inflated by publication bias rather than as the
  expected effect, and does not quote a bias-corrected number it has not checked against the
  source — a response that promises the user a d of 0.65 has failed this item; states
  that automaticity takes weeks to months and varies — no "21 days" folklore; treats retiring a
  habit as success, not failure; and routes drive problems and standardization to
  `coding-agent-skills:stay-hard-accountability` and
  `continuous-improvement-skills:standard-work` instead of absorbing them.
