# Records and documentary research — Ireland, the United States, the platforms, the citations

The documentary layer is where names come from. This file lists the record classes, where each
lives, what is free, and the known gaps, so a research plan can be drawn up before the first
search. Availability moves — cut-off years roll forward, indexes launch, sites close uploads — so
the dated items sit in a ledger with the date they were read, and the rule is to **re-read the
live page at the time of use** and update the ledger. Everything here was compiled from secondary
guides and search excerpts of the named sites unless marked otherwise.

## §1 Ireland

**Civil registration.** Births, marriages and deaths for all religions from 1864; non-Catholic
marriages from 1845 [snippet-only]. Images are free on the state genealogy site under the rolling
**100 / 75 / 50-year rule** (births over 100 years old, marriages over 75, deaths over 50); compute
the expected cut-off from the current year and read the actual figure off the site, because
secondary sources disagree by a year. Known gap: some death-register images for 1864–1871 are
missing [snippet-only]. Northern Ireland's civil records are pay-per-view through its General
Register Office, with a free index that returns only counts [snippet-only].

**Church registers.** The National Library of Ireland's digitised Catholic registers cover roughly
1,086 parishes for baptisms and marriages to 1880, with many western-seaboard registers starting
only in the 1850s–60s; the images are browsable by parish only, and the name indexes belong to the
commercial sites (one launched its index as free-to-search with registration in 2016 — confirm the
current terms) [snippet-only, cross-checked]. The state site's own church records are limited to a
few dioceses; Church of Ireland registers are catalogued by the Representative Church Body
Library; Presbyterian records route through the Public Record Office of Northern Ireland and a
pay-per-view county service [snippet-only].

**Censuses and substitutes.** 1901 and 1911 are complete and free. **The 1926 census (26 counties)
was released free online in April 2026**, with a birthplace update later that year and commercial
transcriptions added [snippet-only, cross-checked — ledger item]. The 1821–1851 censuses were
mostly destroyed; the surviving fragments are listed by county and year, and the 1841/1851 Old Age
Pension search forms are a substitute [snippet-only]. Land substitutes are free: Griffith's
Valuation (1847–1864) with images, the Tithe Applotment Books (1823–1838, omitting towns), and in
the north the indexed Valuation Revision Books [snippet-only].

**The 1922 limit.** The Public Record Office at the Four Courts burned on 30 June 1922, taking
centuries of parish registers, census returns and wills; the Virtual Record Treasury (from 2022)
reconstructs part of it from copies elsewhere [snippet-only]. Pre-1858 wills are gone, with index
substitutes; the 1858–1920 will calendars are free from the National Archives (1921 never filmed),
and the northern calendar runs later with copy-will images for 1858–1900 [snippet-only].

**Deeds, estates, poor law, emigration.** Registry of Deeds memorials from 1708 are free as
unindexed images on FamilySearch for 1708–1929, with a grantor index but no grantee index and a
volunteer name index; the deeds rarely reach tenant farmers [snippet-only]. Landed Estates Court
rentals (1850–1885) name around half a million tenants and sit on the commercial sites; the Land
Commission's records remain effectively closed [snippet-only]. Poor Law records survive unevenly
by union [snippet-only]. For the crossing, the US National Archives' Famine Irish file holds about
600,000 New York arrivals of 1846–1851, free; there are no systematic outward passenger lists
before 1890 [snippet-only]. Royal Irish Constabulary service registers give physical and family
detail for policemen [snippet-only].

**Free-first order:** civil records on the state site → the free Catholic index into the NLI image
→ 1901 / 1911 / 1926 censuses → Griffith's and the Tithe books → will calendars → Registry of
Deeds through the volunteer index; paid access only for estate rentals, poor-law sets, later
passenger lists and loan-fund records.

## §2 United States

**Federal census.** 1790–1950, released under the 72-year rule; the 1950 census opened in April
2022, so the 1960 census is due in April 2032 by the same arithmetic [snippet-only — the rule is
the Census Bureau's; the 2032 date is the inference]. **The 1890 census is effectively lost**: a
1921 fire destroyed about a quarter of the schedules and the remainder was later destroyed; only
a few thousand names survive [snippet-only]. Bridge the gap with state censuses (many states took
them; several never did), city directories and church records [snippet-only]. Some commercial
collections of the later censuses are free with registration [snippet-only].

**Vital records** have no federal index. The method is the CDC's "Where to Write for Vital
Records" guide (state office, fees, start years) plus the FamilySearch Wiki page for the state plus
the state archive — never one database. Statewide registration began at different dates, and
published charts disagree on some states [snippet-only].

**Immigration and naturalisation.** Two free New York portals cover 1820–1957 between them (the
Castle Garden database's live status should be checked; the Ellis Island foundation's search holds
tens of millions of records), with FamilySearch mirroring both periods [snippet-only].
Naturalisation turns on the date of the record: before 27 September 1906 any court could
naturalise with no standard forms, and the National Archives' holdings are usually the declaration
and petition only; afterwards the immigration agency holds duplicate certificate files for
1906–1956, obtained by an index search and then a record request, with fees that have been quoted
at several levels — read the live fee page [snippet-only].

**Military, land, probate, newspapers, directories, deaths.** Civil War pension index and
First World War draft cards (about 24 million men) are free on FamilySearch; the 1942 "old man's"
registration covered men born 1877–1897; Bureau of Land Management patents from 1788 are free
and searchable by name; a very large probate image collection sits behind one commercial site
[snippet-only]. **FamilySearch Full-Text Search**, which reads unindexed handwritten deed, probate
and court books, moved from its labs area to the main search around August 2025 [snippet-only —
ledger item] and is the highest-leverage free US tool for the immigrant period. City directories
trace residence year by year (commercial set 1822–1995; Library of Congress digitised pre-1976
directories for some states) [snippet-only]. Chronicling America relaunched inside the Library of
Congress site in August 2025, covering 1690–1963 with most pages 1836–1922 [snippet-only — ledger
item]; two commercial newspaper archives are the paid complements. The Social Security Death
Index covers 1935–2014 but thinly before 1962; the applications-and-claims index adds parents'
names [snippet-only]. Grave sites: one commercial-owned, one with a free tier [snippet-only].

## §4 Surname distribution as a search strategy

For an Irish surname, the 1890 birth-registration count by province and principal counties
(Matheson's report, free online) gives the first geographic filter; Griffith's Valuation household
counts by parish give the second; and the published sept histories tell you whether the name was
borne by more than one unrelated family [snippet-only]. Where the septs are geographically
distinct, **a county of origin from a US record — a naturalisation, an obituary, a headstone, a
passenger list — is the single most valuable documentary fact**, and it is the fact the Y-DNA
evidence can then test: a terminal SNP that surname projects place with one sept excludes the
others, a discrimination surname distribution alone cannot make. Anglicised spellings vary by
family and decade; search every variant.

## §5 Citation templates

Layered form (original → provider → "citing"), after *Evidence Explained*; these are
**constructions to check against the fourth-edition templates**, not quotations from the book.

- *Irish civil record image:* record type, name, date, registrar's district, Superintendent
  Registrar's District, county, entry number; "digital image," site name, collection name (URL :
  accessed date), the site's group registration identifier.
- *NLI Catholic register:* parish and diocese, register type, date and entry; "digital image,"
  National Library of Ireland, Catholic Parish Registers (URL : accessed date), microfilm and
  page reference; "citing" the parish's original register where the site states it.
- *Griffith's Valuation:* county, barony, parish, townland, householder, year of publication;
  "digital image," site name (URL : accessed date).
- *Irish census 1901/1911/1926:* county, district electoral division, townland or street, house
  number, household head, form; "digital image," National Archives of Ireland (URL : accessed
  date).
- *US federal census:* state, county, place, year, schedule, enumeration district, sheet,
  dwelling and family numbers, household head; "digital image," provider (URL : accessed date);
  "citing" the National Archives microfilm publication and roll. (A journal style sheet of
  December 2025 omits "population schedule" and spells out "enumeration district" at first use
  only [snippet-only].)
- *DNA match:* tester (privatised), match (privatised), company, test type, date viewed; total cM,
  longest segment, segment count, X; the tool and version consulted for the range and probability.
