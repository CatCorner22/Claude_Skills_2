# Ulysses pact (bind the future self)

Odysseus's move was not resisting the Sirens — it was knowing in advance that he would
not be able to, and arranging the world so that his degraded future self lacked the
authority to act. The clinical descendant is real and legally instantiated: psychiatric
advance directives let a person, while well, pre-authorize treatment they know their
crisis self will refuse — the literature calls these Ulysses contracts [peer-reviewed,
PubMed/PMC-sourced] — and behavioral economics generalizes the move as the commitment
device. This skill writes that instrument for everyday judgment: the rules your calm
self adopts against the pressured self it can see coming.

## When to use
- A rule keeps getting broken by its own author under a predictable pressure: the
  midnight force-push, the "just this once" deploy on close day, the angry reply sent
  before morning, doubling down when the sunk-cost fog rolls in.
- A degradation window is foreseeable — a launch, a market event, a negotiation, an
  on-call rotation — and the defaults for it should be chosen now, while nobody is in it.
- Enforcement moment: the user is about to do the thing a registered pact bars, and the
  assistant's job is to produce the pact — their own words, their own reasons, the
  adoption date — and let the calm self argue with the pressured one.
- A review date arrives and the pact should be kept, resized, or retired deliberately.
- In prose, the institutional siblings: dual-approval and second-signature regimes are
  organizational mast-lashing — the same mechanism at institutional scale. This skill
  stays domain-neutral and personal-to-team scale; wire the pattern into whatever
  payment, deploy, or change controls your environment already has.
- Not for: pre-authoring emergency *action* — tripwires, first moves, pre-granted
  expiring authority for when a crisis hits → see
  `safety-and-reliability-skills:break-glass-playbooks`. The pact is its
  restraint-side twin: it pre-positions *refusal*, not action; together they cover
  both halves of "decide it now, while calm."
- Not for: revisiting whether a committed plan still deserves continuation when
  evidence changes → see `decision-science-skills:the-challenger`. The seam is a
  partnership: the-challenger *revisits* commitments under momentum; this skill
  *creates* binding ones against a foreseen worse self — and a good pact names the
  evidence that should send you to the-challenger instead of holding the mast.
- Not for: making a wanted routine automatic → see `learning-skills:habit-design`.
  Habits automate the behavior you want; pacts bar the behavior you don't, under
  pressures where no routine survives.

## Do it
The interview script, pact template, enforcement etiquette, renegotiation protocol, and
failure-mode table are in `references/pact-method.md`.

1. **Interview for degradation, in a calm state.** The assistant runs the
   degradation-scenario interview: where has your judgment predictably gone bad before?
   For each situation, capture the tell (the observable early sign), the typical bad
   move, and what it cost last time. Only situations with a repeat pattern earn a pact —
   this is an instrument against the *foreseen* worse self, not all possible ones.
2. **Draft the pact, one rule at a time.** Each pact is one testable rule bound to a
   trigger context, plus its machinery: a required second signature for exceptions
   (someone who will actually refuse), a cooling-off period before the barred act, and a
   pre-committed default (what happens when no decision is made in time). Narrow beats
   broad — a pact that fires constantly gets ignored.
3. **Pre-decide the unbinding criteria.** Write down, now, what evidence would
   legitimately release the pact — the real change in the world under which the rule no
   longer serves its purpose. A pact without release criteria is a straitjacket, and
   straitjackets get cut off: the first legitimate exception becomes the precedent that
   kills the whole instrument.
4. **Register the pact where the future self will hit it.** A pact registered nowhere
   binds no one. Put it in the path of the act: the PR template, the runbook step, the
   deploy checklist item, the email-draft ritual, a pacts file the assistant reads — and
   tell the assistant to enforce on request. Record the adoption date and a short "why"
   paragraph in the calm self's own words; that paragraph is the enforcement payload.
5. **Set the review date.** Pacts are renegotiated deliberately, in calm state, on
   schedule — never under fire. An unreviewed pact drifts into clutter; a pact
   renegotiated mid-crisis was never a pact.
6. **Enforce with quotation, never scolding.** When the moment comes, the assistant
   says: *"You adopted a rule against exactly this on <date>. Here is what you wrote,
   and why."* — the pact verbatim, the why paragraph, the exception machinery (second
   signature, cooling-off), and nothing else. The calm self does the persuading; the
   assistant just delivers the letter. Overrides happen only through the pact's own
   exception path, and every near-miss is logged for the review date.

**Division of labor.** The assistant interviews, drafts, registers, quotes, and logs;
the human owns every rule, every unbinding criterion, and every override. A pact the
user did not author in their own words has no authority at 11pm.

## Why / learn
Commitment devices work because judgment degradation is predictable in *kind* even when
unpredictable in timing. You cannot know which night the production incident comes, but
you can know — from your own record — what 11pm-you does under one. That asymmetry is
the whole instrument: the calm self has better judgment but no presence at the moment of
action; the pressured self has presence but degraded judgment. A pact is how the calm
self attends the moment anyway: decisions made early, when the deciding is good, and
delivered late, when it is needed. Psychiatry's version makes the logic vivid — the
patient drafting an advance directive knows, clinically and personally, that the crisis
self will refuse treatment, and binds against that specific foreseen refusal
[peer-reviewed]. Everyday pacts are the same move at lower stakes.

The unbinding criteria are what separate a pact from a straitjacket, and the difference
is load-bearing. A rule with no legitimate exit forces its author to choose, at the
worst moment, between the rule and reality — and reality wins, taking the rule's
credibility with it. Pre-deciding the release evidence keeps the exit *owned by the calm
self*: the pressured self can still get out, but only through a door the calm self
built, on evidence the calm self named. The same logic gives the cooling-off period its
power — it does not forbid the act, it only moves it past the window where the
degradation peaks; most angry emails do not survive their own cooling-off.

Enforcement etiquette matters because the enforcer the future self resents is the
enforcer the future self routes around. Scolding converts the pact into an adversary
and the pressured self into a lawyer against it. Quotation converts it into a letter
from someone the pressured self already trusts — themselves. That is also why the "why"
paragraph is written at adoption time: the rule alone is a fence, but the reasons are
the calm self's voice, and it is the voice that persuades.

## Common mistakes
- Pact too broad ("never deploy without full sign-off") → fires constantly, becomes
  noise, dies → bind the rule to the narrow trigger context where judgment actually
  degrades.
- No unbinding clause → the first legitimate exception kills the instrument → pre-decide
  the release evidence at adoption.
- Registered nowhere → the future self never encounters it → put it in the path of the
  act, and give the assistant the enforcement job explicitly.
- Written under fire → that is a reaction wearing a pact's clothes; it binds the wrong
  self → wait for calm, then draft.
- Second signature from someone who always says yes → a rubber stamp is not a mast →
  pick a signer who has refused you before.
- Enforcement by scolding → the future self hides from the enforcer → quote the pact and
  the why; let the calm self argue.
- Renegotiating mid-crisis → the moment the pact exists for is the moment it cannot be
  renegotiated → log the objection for the review date and hold.
- Pacts multiplying past memory → ten pacts bind less than three → consolidate at each
  review; retire what no longer earns its place.

## Tailor to your environment
Record in `references/your-environment.md`: your registered pacts (or where the pact
file lives), your known degradation windows, who your real second signatories are, your
registration points (PR template, runbook, checklist step), and your review cadence.
Keep committed content structural — actual pact texts about sensitive matters, named
signatories, or anything client-related belongs in `your-environment.private.md`
(git-ignored), never in a committed file.
