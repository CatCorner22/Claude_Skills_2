# The habit method: worksheet, patterns, audits, renegotiation

Research base: Gollwitzer's implementation-intentions work and the psychology of automaticity
and context-dependent behavior. On effect size, be careful: Gollwitzer & Sheeran's 2006
meta-analysis reported d ≈ 0.65 across 94 independent tests of pre-deciding the when/where/how
of acting, and that number is quoted everywhere — including by people selling habit systems.
The literature has substantial publication bias, and bias-corrected re-analyses put the effect
materially below the headline figure; the direction of the correction is not in dispute even
where the exact magnitude is. **Specific corrected values are `[unverified here]`** — this
environment could not reach the primary sources, and a previous revision of this file quoted a
"corrected" band that a reviewer demonstrated sat above the source's own bias-corrected estimate,
i.e. the correction itself was overstated. The defensible claim is qualitative: the effect is
real, it is smaller than 0.65, and writing the plan costs almost nothing — which is why the
procedure keeps it. Do not put a number on it in front of a user without checking the source.

Gollwitzer's canonical form is if-then ("if situation Y, then I will do X"); "when X, I will
Y" below is the same structure in plainer words. Fogg (*Tiny Habits*) and Clear (*Atomic
Habits*) are practitioner syntheses used here for vocabulary, not as research authority.

## The design worksheet

One worksheet per habit; the assistant fills it with the user and reads it back at check-ins.

```
Habit name:            <short handle>
Six-month "why":       <what this behavior is in service of>
Tiny routine (v1):     <the smallest version that counts>
Scale target (later):  <the eventual full size — not the starting requirement>
Cue:                   <anchor habit or specific event/place>
Implementation
  intention (verbatim):"When <cue>, I will <tiny routine> in <place>."
Coping plan:           "If <known obstacle>, then <planned response>."
Immediate reward /
  closure signal:      <what marks 'done', felt now>
Friction changes:      good path −1 step: <what gets staged, when>
                       bad path  +1 step: <what gets moved/hidden/logged out>
Tracker location:      <where the presence record lives>
Stability gate:        <e.g., ~90% presence over 3–4 weeks before scaling>
Renegotiation date:    <calendar date, 6–8 weeks out>
```

## Implementation-intention patterns

The load-bearing property is *situational concreteness*: the "when" is an event the user
will unmistakably notice, and the "will" is a single concrete act.

- **Starting:** "When I <existing event>, I will <tiny act> in <place>."
  — "When I close the 9:30 stand-up, I will write one line in the rep log at my desk."
- **Stopping (as replacement):** cues can't be deleted, only re-pointed. "When I feel the
  urge to <old routine> / when <old cue> happens, I will <replacement act> instead."
  — "When I reach for my phone in the elevator, I will open the flashcard deck instead."
- **Coping (protect the habit from its known obstacle):** "If <obstacle>, then <response>."
  — "If I'm traveling, then the routine is one card in the hotel room — that counts."
- **Resumption (pre-decide the post-miss move):** "If I miss a day, then tomorrow I do only
  the tiny version and note the cause in the tracker." This one sentence is the structural
  antidote to the day-zero collapse.

Weak forms to reject when drafting: aspirations ("I'll be more consistent"), frequency vows
("three times a week" with no cue), and any "when" the user has to remember to notice.

## Choosing anchors for habit stacking

An anchor is an existing behavior the new routine attaches after. Good anchors are:

- **Already automatic** — happens without deliberation (first coffee, arriving at desk,
  closing the laptop for the day). A wobbly anchor transmits its wobble.
- **Right frequency** — a daily habit needs a daily anchor; stacking a daily routine on a
  weekly event starves it.
- **Right context** — the anchor should end where the new routine can physically start; an
  anchor in the car can't cue a routine that needs the desk.
- **Precisely bounded** — "after I pour the coffee," not "during breakfast." The routine
  fires at the anchor's *edge*.

Chains are possible (anchor → habit A → habit B) once the earlier link is itself stable —
each stabilized habit becomes a legal anchor for the next.

## The friction audit

List the actual steps, count them, then move one.

1. Walk the good path from cue to done, step by step, honestly ("get up, find the shoes,
   find the socks, …"). Every step is a place the behavior can die.
2. Remove or pre-stage one step, the night or the session before: worksheet open on the
   second monitor, deck on the home screen, gear laid out.
3. Walk the competing path the same way, and *add* one step to it: logged out, app off the
   home screen, television remote in the drawer, the snack not purchased at all (the
   supermarket is the cheapest place to exercise willpower — once, not nightly).
4. Re-audit whenever the habit starts slipping: friction creeps back silently, and a
   slipping habit is more often a re-friction event than a motivation event.

The asymmetry to exploit: at the moment of the cue, the marginal step decides. Nobody's
resolve is reliably worth one extra flight of stairs at 6 a.m.; the audit makes sure it
never has to be.

## Presence tracking

```
| Date | Present? | Size done | Note (only if missed or odd) |
|------|----------|-----------|------------------------------|
| ...  | y        | tiny      |                              |
| ...  | n        | —         | cue never fired — stand-up cancelled |
```

- **Presence rate**, not streak length, is the health metric — e.g., "18 of last 21 days" =
  86%, which is under a ~90% stability gate, so the honest call is hold the size, don't scale.
- One diagnostic question per miss: did the cue fire? was the routine too big that day? did
  friction reappear? The answer feeds redesign, not judgment.
- Two consecutive misses → treat as a design signal: shrink the routine one notch or move
  the cue to a more reliable anchor. Silent, guilt-driven "trying harder" is the one
  response the data never supports.
- The tiny version always counts as present. Recording "tiny" on a bad day is the system
  working, not a lesser mark.

## The renegotiation protocol

At the date set in the worksheet (and after any major context change — new job, move, new
schedule):

1. **Read the tracker.** Presence rate, miss pattern, size actually done.
2. **Re-ask the six-month why.** Is this habit still in service of anything?
3. **Decide, deliberately, one of:**
   - **Keep** as is (still earning its slot);
   - **Scale** one notch (stability gate met and appetite exists);
   - **Shrink** (presence below the gate — the honest fix is smaller, not harder);
   - **Re-cue** (routine is fine, cue is unreliable or its context changed);
   - **Retire** (purpose served or gone). Retirement is a success state: the worksheet is
     closed with a one-line epitaph, and the design slot opens for the next habit.
4. **Set the next renegotiation date.** Habits are tenancies, not tattoos.

## Failure-mode table

| Failure mode | Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Too big | Skipped on any low-energy day; dread before the cue | Shrink below the failure threshold; record the ambition as the scale target |
| No cue / vague cue | "I just forget"; done at random times or not at all | Bind to a specific, already-automatic anchor's edge |
| Reward too distant | Executes for a week on enthusiasm, then decays | Attach an immediate closure signal; reserve a small pleasure for after |
| Environment fights it | Done only with visible effort; misses cluster in one place/time | Friction audit; re-stage the room before re-asking the person |
| Too many at once | Several new habits all at ~50% presence | Pick one; park the rest, in writing, for after its stability gate |
| Streak framing | One miss followed by abandonment | Presence tracking; the pre-written resumption intention |
| Cue fires when acting is impossible | Cue at a moment with no time/place for the routine | Move the cue, or split: capture-now ("note it"), do-later at a second cue |
| Context change broke it | Solid habit vanished after travel/move/reorg | Not a relapse — re-run the design against the new context; re-cue |
