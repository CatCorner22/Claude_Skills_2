# Voice mechanics — sentence-level gonzo craft

Contents: 1. The four registers, worked · 2. Rhythm and punctuation · 3. Diction
families · 4. The devices · 5. Openings · 6. Closings · 7. Register mixing map ·
8. Heat calibration

All examples below are ORIGINAL, written for this file about invented or generic
subjects. They demonstrate the style; they quote nothing. Attested Thompson quotes
live in gonzo-canon.md with sources.

## 1. The four registers, worked

**High-velocity invective** — stacked clauses, escalating specificity, aimed at power:
> The subcommittee had the eyes of men who have eaten too well for too long off other
> people's futures — sleek, blinkless, faintly annoyed that the public exists — and
> when the chairman gaveled us into what he called "an open dialogue," three
> stenographers flinched at once, like gulls sensing weather.

Mechanics: one long sentence carrying three escalations; the concrete flinch at the
end grounds it. Invective without a grounding image is just noise.

**Paranoid escalation** — one true detail, inflated stepwise, inflation visible:
> The ice machine in the mezzanine had been OUT OF ORDER for three days. Fine.
> Machines fail. But the sign was laminated — laminated, as if someone had planned for
> a long siege — and by Thursday I understood that nobody in that hotel expected the
> ice to return in our lifetime, and that they had made a separate peace with this,
> as people do, one small surrender at a time, which is exactly how the big
> surrenders get their paperwork done.

Mechanics: the reader watches a laminated sign become a thesis about civilizational
decay and enjoys the ride precisely because the inflation is honest about itself.

**Deadpan procedural** — flat, fake-precise logistics that make the wild register land:
> Expenses, day two: one (1) rental sedan, beige, demoralizing. Four (4) coffees,
> three abandoned. One (1) laminated agenda, annotated until illegible. Parking:
> contested, pending, possibly criminal.

Mechanics: inventory format, parenthetical counts, the last item swerving. The
procedural register is the straight man; schedule it before or after a fever passage.

**The elegiac wave** — the mask off, once per dispatch, plain and mournful:
> There was a moment, maybe twenty minutes in, when the youngest board member asked
> the only honest question of the night — you could hear it land, the room went
> quiet the way rooms do when something true gets loose in them — and for about
> eight seconds the whole tired machine remembered what it was for. Then the counsel
> table coughed, the moment closed over, and we went back to the agenda, all of us,
> together, like water closing over a stone.

Mechanics: no irony, no inflation, longer breath, one image held to the end. This
passage is why the rest gets to be wild.

## 2. Rhythm and punctuation
- The em-dash is the native connective — thought interrupting thought — and three in a
  sentence is a rhythm, not an error, IF the sentence lands on a short hard clause.
- Alternate sentence lengths violently: a 60-word runner, then four words. The short
  sentence is the punch; the long one is the wind-up.
- Ellipses signal the narrator losing the thread on purpose... then snapping back.
- ALL-CAPS for one or two words maximum — a shout, not a setting.

## 3. Diction families
- **Predator/animal**: power as reptiles, swine, vultures, things with mandibles.
- **Medical/clinical**: dread diagnosed like a condition ("a low-grade professional
  despair, possibly terminal").
- **Legal/procedural**: liability, exhibits, counsel, "pending litigation" as comic
  texture — doubly available given who this Desk's counsel is.
- **Weather/apocalypse**: fronts moving in, tides going out, "high-water mark" as the
  load-bearing elegiac image.
- **Doomed logistics**: everything is late, rented, leaking, or laminated.
- Capitalized Abstractions used sparingly: the American Dream, Progress, Doom, the
  Machine. One per dispatch does more than five.

## 4. The devices
- **Interpolated artifacts**: a fake telegram to an editor, an itemized expense list, a
  memo from management — formatted as the artifact, dropped mid-dispatch.
- **The retained-counsel aside**: the Desk consults its attorney in-text. House
  inversion: counsel is the user, real, and gets the good lines. ("Counsel has
  reviewed this paragraph and responded with a single word, billed at six minutes.")
- **Sudden second person**: mid-passage, turn on the reader — "and you would have done
  the same thing, don't pretend otherwise" — once, maybe twice.
- **The credential motif**: the press pass, the badge, the wristband that doesn't
  work — standing symbol for the whole relationship between observer and machine.
- **Time-stamping**: "4:30 a.m." datelines create the deadline-pressure frame even in
  a Tuesday-afternoon dispatch.

## 5. Openings
Never with the event. Open with: the journey in medias res ("Somewhere past the second
security checkpoint the optimism gave out"), the artifact (the agenda, the invitation,
the summons), or the false calm ("It was supposed to be a routine meeting. They are
always supposed to be routine."). The subject enters late, already surrounded.

## 6. Closings
- **Hot**: velocity out — an exit in motion, unresolved, the noise still rising.
- **Hollow**: the comedown inventory — the empty room, the stacked chairs, the bill.
- Test: if the final paragraph could be pasted into a memo's "conclusion" section,
  delete it and end on the image above it.

## 7. Register mixing map
A serviceable 800-word dispatch: open procedural or in-medias-res (¶1) → invective
build (¶2–3) → paranoid escalation on one detail (¶4) → artifact interpolation (¶5) →
counsel aside (¶6) → the wave (¶7) → hot or hollow close (¶8). Vary it; never omit
the wave; never let two fever passages touch without a flat one between.

## 8. Heat calibration
Three settings, chosen by the user's venue (defaults in your-environment.md):
- **Simmer**: no profanity, full irony — survives a bar-association newsletter.
- **Rolling boil**: mild profanity, open invective — private circulation, blogs.
- **Full gonzo**: the user asked for it by name and owns the venue.
All settings keep the same rails: fiction only in the narrator's head, hyperbole
cognizable as hyperbole, punches aimed up.
