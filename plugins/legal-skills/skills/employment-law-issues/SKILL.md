# Employment-law and HR issue-spotting (with live-source verification)

Employment law is the field where "I read the rule once" fails fastest: the thresholds are
statutory but the agency positions, the state overlays and the salary figures move, sometimes
mid-year, sometimes by a court vacating a rule that still reads as current in every cached copy.
So this skill does not try to be the rulebook. It is an issue-spotting method with a verification
discipline: place the situation on the lifecycle map, name every issue and the source that governs
it, verify the live text before relying on a number, assess exposure, and deliver the result the
way a decision-maker reads it — exposure first.

## When to use
- An HR or employment question from an employer, a manager, or an employee: pay, leave, a
  complaint, a classification, a termination, a policy.
- Reviewing an offer letter, employment agreement, separation or severance agreement, restrictive
  covenant, or handbook for the issues it raises (then `legal-skills:mscd-contract-drafting` for
  the words).
- Planning a workplace investigation or a termination.
- Not for: resumes, interviews, salary negotiation or job-search coaching (a career-coaching skill,
  if installed, owns those). Researching and verifying the authorities an issue turns on →
  `legal-skills:legal-citation-research`. Drafting the agreement's language →
  `legal-skills:mscd-contract-drafting`.

## Do it

1. **Get the facts that set the thresholds.** Employer headcount (and where it is counted);
   every state and locality where the people involved work, including remote workers; union or
   collective-bargaining status; federal-contractor status; industry; the worker's classification
   on paper; dates (hire, complaint, leave, discipline, termination); and the documents in play
   (offer letter, agreement, handbook, policies, personnel file, communications). Most thresholds
   in `references/lifecycle-issue-checklist.md` turn on the first two items.

2. **Place the situation on the lifecycle map and run the checklist for that stage.** Hiring →
   classification → wage and hour → leave and accommodation → discrimination and harassment →
   performance and discipline → investigation → termination and severance → restrictive covenants
   → handbooks and policies → multi-state and remote → privacy and data. Each stage's table names
   the issues, the governing sources, what to verify live, what to pull, and the counsel triggers.
   `references/lifecycle-issue-checklist.md`.

3. **Name the governing source for every issue, by layer.** Federal statute and agency; state
   statute and agency; local ordinance; the contract, handbook or collective agreement; and the
   employer's own past practice (consistency is evidence). Write the source next to the issue. A
   number without its instrument is a rumour.

4. **Verify currency before relying on any number or agency position.** Open the live source —
   the official code, *ecfr.gov*, the agency's current page, the state statute — and date the
   check. `references/moving-parts-ledger.md` lists what is known to have moved recently and the
   route to check each; two of its items (the FTC non-compete rule, the 2024 overtime threshold)
   are rules that were vacated and still read as current in older material. Pass anything that
   needs authority to `legal-skills:legal-citation-research`.

5. **Assess exposure, including the one that is usually missed.** For each issue: likelihood,
   severity, and whether it can still be fixed. Then check the **timing against protected
   activity** — a complaint, a leave request, an accommodation request, a wage inquiry, a report
   to an agency — because retaliation is the claim most often made and the one a clean underlying
   decision does not cure. Record deadlines that are already running (agency charge windows,
   notice periods, final-pay timing, revocation periods). `references/lifecycle-issue-checklist.md` §6.

6. **Build the action plan in three horizons.** *Now*: preserve documents (and a litigation hold
   if a claim is foreseeable), interim measures, stop the clock where you can. *Next*: the
   investigation or the interactive process, run to protocol. *Decide*: the termination or policy
   decision, with the checklist worked and the consistency check done. Name the counsel triggers:
   any threat of a charge or suit, protected-activity timing, a group or pattern, immigration,
   union activity, an executive or a multi-state covenant.
   `references/investigation-and-termination-playbook.md`.

7. **Deliver the Smart Brevity issue list.** Exposure first — the single biggest issue in one
   sentence — then why it matters, do now, decisions needed, verify list (every number with its
   source and check date), counsel triggers, and the ledger. For a document review, add the
   provision-by-provision table. `references/issue-list-template.md`.

8. **Hand off the words and the law.** Clause language goes to
   `legal-skills:mscd-contract-drafting`; any authority the answer rests on goes through
   `legal-skills:legal-citation-research` before it is cited. This skill spots and organizes; it
   does not draft the release or verify the case.

## Why / learn

**Why issue-spotting beats rule-knowing here.** A memorised rule has a date on it, and employment
rules move in three ways at once: statutes are amended, agencies reverse their own precedents and
guidance when administrations change, and states add floors and prohibitions the federal scheme
does not have. A method that names the issue and the source, and then verifies, survives all
three. A method that remembers the number does not.

**Why retaliation sits in its own step.** The underlying complaint may be weak and the employer
may be right about the performance problem, and none of that matters if the termination follows
the complaint closely enough for a factfinder to infer cause. Protected activity turns ordinary
management decisions into claims; the defence is documentation that predates the activity,
consistency with how others were treated, and process that is visibly the same.

**Why process is the product.** The interactive process for an accommodation, a prompt and
impartial investigation, a performance plan with real expectations, and a termination checklist
are not formalities; they are the evidence that the decision was made for the stated reason.
Courts and agencies ask what the employer did, in what order, and whether it did the same for
everyone.

**Why the thresholds are marked and dated.** Every number in this skill's references is either
[rule text] with its instrument named or [snippet-only] with a check date, because the one thing a
reader will do with a threshold is rely on it. The marks are the instruction: open the source.

**Why Smart Brevity.** The reader is an owner, a general counsel or an HR director deciding
whether to call outside counsel today. The exposure comes first, in one sentence; the law comes
after, in the ledger.

## Common mistakes
- Deciding on the federal rule alone → the state or city rule is often stricter (minimum wage,
  leave, non-competes, final pay, harassment training, pay transparency); check every place the
  people work.
- Using a salary threshold, a leave entitlement or an agency position from memory or a 2024
  article → both of the ledger's headline items changed after being published; verify live and
  date it.
- Treating a severance or confidentiality clause as boilerplate → NLRB positions on
  confidentiality and non-disparagement in severance agreements have moved and are under
  reconsideration again; whistleblower and agency-charge carve-outs are required by other rules.
- Terminating after a complaint without a timing check → the retaliation claim is independent of
  whether the complaint had merit.
- Classifying by title or by what the worker wants → exemption and contractor status turn on
  duties, control and economic reality under tests that differ by statute and state.
- Skipping the interactive process because the answer seems obvious → the process is the defence.
- Running an investigation without interim measures, a plan, or documentation → an investigation
  that cannot be shown is one that did not happen.
- Promising confidentiality or outcomes to a complainant or a witness → promise process and
  anti-retaliation, not results.
- Giving a number without its instrument → a reader will rely on it.

## Tailor to your environment
Record in `references/your-environment.md`: the employer profile (headcount by state, union
status, contractor status, industry); the policies and templates in force and their last review
dates; the outside-counsel escalation path; the deadlines calendar; which state overlays apply and
where you verify them; and the moving-parts items you have checked, with dates. Keep anything
identifying a person, a complaint or a matter in the `.private.md` twin.

**Keep your filled-in copy outside the plugin.** This file ships as a *template* and lives inside
the installed plugin, where a `/plugin marketplace update` can overwrite it or refuse to run against
a dirty tree. Copy it into your own project — `.claude/skills-env/employment-law-issues.private.md` works well — fill it in
there, and point this skill at that copy. Your specifics then survive updates and stay somewhere you
own rather than in a cache you may not realise is disposable.
