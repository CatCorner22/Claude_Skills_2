# Investigation and termination playbook

Contents: §1 The investigation protocol · §2 The termination checklist · §3 The separation-agreement
checklist · §4 Communications: what to say and not say

## §1 The investigation protocol

A complaint that is not investigated promptly, impartially and visibly is the employer's problem
twice: once for the conduct, once for the response. The protocol is the evidence that the response
happened.

1. **Intake and preservation (day 0).** Record what was reported, by whom, when, and how. Preserve
   the relevant records (email, chat, badge and system logs, personnel files); issue a litigation
   hold if a claim is foreseeable. Thank the complainant, explain the process, and state the
   anti-retaliation rule in writing.
2. **Interim measures (day 0–1).** Separate the parties if needed without burdening the
   complainant (schedule or reporting-line changes fall on the accused or are neutral; paid
   administrative leave for the accused where warranted). Record what was done and why.
3. **Scope and investigator (day 1–2).** Define the allegations in writing. Choose an investigator
   with no stake in the outcome and the standing to interview everyone involved; counsel-directed
   where privilege may matter, with *Upjohn* warnings in every interview [canon attribution].
   Outside investigator for a senior accused, a complaint against HR, or an agency charge already
   filed.
4. **Plan.** Who to interview and in what order (complainant, witnesses, accused last, follow-ups);
   which documents to collect; the timeline; the standard (preponderance).
5. **Interviews.** Two people present; open questions; no promises of confidentiality beyond "as
   confidential as the process allows" — a blanket confidentiality instruction to non-supervisory
   employees can collide with their rights to discuss working conditions, and the Board's standard
   on confidentiality during an open investigation has moved more than once (ledger §2, the
   *Apogee Retail* row) [background — verify]. Record each interview the same day. Ask each witness about
   retaliation and how to reach them.
6. **Findings.** For each allegation: substantiated, not substantiated, inconclusive — with the
   evidence relied on and credibility reasoning (consistency, corroboration, motive, demeanour
   used cautiously). Policy violation and legal conclusion are different findings; the report
   makes the first and leaves the second to counsel.
7. **Remediation.** Proportionate to the finding and consistent with past practice; recorded.
8. **Closure.** Tell the complainant and the accused that the investigation is complete and what
   action was taken in general terms; repeat the anti-retaliation rule; set a follow-up check-in
   with the complainant at 30 and 90 days and record it.
9. **Retention.** The file, segregated from personnel files, retained per policy and any hold.

## §2 The termination checklist

Work every line before the meeting, and keep the worked checklist in the file.

- [ ] **Stated reason**, in one sentence, supported by documents that predate the decision.
- [ ] **Consistency**: comparable conduct by others, and what happened to them. A difference needs
      a documented reason.
- [ ] **Timing against protected activity**: complaints, leave or accommodation requests, wage
      inquiries, safety reports, agency contact, union activity, whistleblowing — anything in the
      past year. If there is any, counsel before the decision.
- [ ] **Contract and policy**: an employment agreement with cause, notice or severance terms; a
      handbook promise of progressive discipline; a collective agreement with just-cause and
      grievance steps.
- [ ] **Protected status check** (not a reason to act or not act — a reason to document the
      legitimate reason carefully): age 40 and over, disability or recent medical leave, pregnancy,
      recent return from military service, any class under state law.
- [ ] **Group reductions**: WARN and mini-WARN headcounts and notice; selection criteria written
      before selection; adverse-impact review of the selected group; OWBPA group disclosures.
- [ ] **Final pay**: state timing (some states: on the day for involuntary terminations); accrued
      vacation payout per state law and policy; commissions and bonuses earned; expense
      reimbursement; no unlawful deductions.
- [ ] **Benefits**: COBRA notice timing; plan-based rights; equity vesting and post-termination
      exercise windows.
- [ ] **Separation agreement** (if any): §3.
- [ ] **Logistics**: the meeting (two people, short, the reason stated once, no debate); system
      access and return of property; the written notice; the reference policy; unemployment
      response.
- [ ] **The file**: the decision memo, the checklist, the comparators, the timeline.

## §3 The separation-agreement checklist

| Element | What to check | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Consideration | something beyond what the employee is already owed (final pay and accrued leave are owed) | contract law; state wage law |
| Release scope | claims through the signing date; known and unknown claims with the state-required language where applicable (California Civil Code § 1542 waiver) [rule text]; no release of claims that cannot be released (unemployment, workers' compensation, vested benefits, future claims) | state law |
| Carve-outs | the right to file an agency charge and participate in an investigation (with a waiver of monetary recovery where permitted); whistleblower rights and awards (SEC Rule 21F-17) [canon attribution]; rights to discuss working conditions (NLRA § 7) [rule text] | federal and state law |
| OWBPA (age 40 and over) | plain language; written advice to consult a lawyer; 21 days to consider (45 for a group program, plus the written disclosure of the decisional unit, eligibility factors, job titles and ages); 7 days to revoke after signing; the release refers to the ADEA | 29 U.S.C. § 626(f) [rule text] |
| Confidentiality and non-disparagement | narrow, with the carve-outs above; a broad clause for a non-supervisory employee is at risk under *McLaren Macomb*, which is under reconsideration before a Board majority able to overrule it (ledger §2) [snippet-only]; sexual-harassment pre-dispute NDAs limited by the Speak Out Act and state laws [canon attribution; background — verify] | NLRA; federal and state statutes |
| Trade secrets | DTSA whistleblower-immunity notice if the agreement governs confidential information (18 U.S.C. § 1833(b)) [rule text] | DTSA |
| No-rehire, cooperation, return of property, transition | state limits on no-rehire clauses in some states [background — verify]; cooperation clauses with reimbursement | state law |
| Tax and Medicare | withholding on severance; reporting; Medicare secondary-payer reporting where a medical claim is released [background — verify] | IRS; CMS |
| Governing law and forum | consistent with where the employee works and the state's limits on choice of law for employees | state law |
| Execution | signature timing after the termination date where OWBPA applies; the revocation period honoured before payment | OWBPA |

## §4 Communications: what to say and not say

- **To the employee:** the decision, the effective date, the final-pay and benefits facts, the
  agreement if there is one and the time to consider it. The reason stated once in neutral terms.
  No debate, no new reasons, no comments on protected characteristics or activity.
- **To the complainant and witnesses:** process, not promises; anti-retaliation in writing; a
  named contact.
- **To the team:** the departure and the transition only.
- **To references:** the policy (dates, title) unless a signed authorization says otherwise.
- **In writing, anywhere:** only what you would be content to read aloud in a hearing.
