# Evals — continuous-improvement-skills:standard-work

## 1. Positive trigger (should load the skill)
> "Every person on our team does the weekly status snapshot differently and the results vary —
> and the one person who really knows the pitfalls is leaving next month. Can you help me
> write standard work / an SOP for it so it's done the same way every time?"

Expected: skill loads; scopes one repeatable task with its trigger and end condition;
documents the current best method observed at the gemba (what actually works, not the
aspiration); records the Toyota trio — sequence, timing with takt/volume context, and
standard inputs/WIP; captures TWI-style key points *with the reason for each* (this is where
the departing expert's tribal knowledge lands); adds visual management; trains with the
four-step do-it-and-explain-it verification; and sets a review cadence with a deviation/idea
path — framing the SOP as a living baseline for improvement.

## 2. Near-miss (should NOT load this skill)
> "Help me build the month-end close calendar — sequence all the tasks across the team with
> owners and business-day offsets."

Expected: this is sequencing a whole multi-task close calendar — a scheduling task, not
documenting one repeatable task's method — so standard-work should NOT load (the close
calendar build was owned by the archived `accounting-skills:month-end-close`; if the ask were
which close task gates the calendar, `continuous-improvement-skills:theory-of-constraints`
is the neighbor). If standard-work loads instead, tighten the cross-links. (Standardizing
one close task's method, though, is a legitimate standard-work use.)

## 2b. Near-miss (checklist guard)
> "Our deployment SOP is fine — people just skip the two steps that matter when they're
> rushed. Can you boil it down to a card they'll actually use?"

Expected: distilling an existing standard to the few killer items people skip is
`safety-and-reliability-skills:checklist-design`. Standard-work documents the full method;
the checklist rides on top of it. If standard-work loads as the primary skill, the seam is
failing.

## 3. Quality rubric
A good response:
- **Does the task:** produces standard work for one task with sequence, timing (takt/cycle
  or honest volume context), standard inputs, key points *and their reasons* in the TWI
  breakdown format, visual management, four-step training with do-and-explain verification,
  and a review cadence plus deviation path.
- **Teaches:** explains *why* you can't improve an unstable process, that the standard is
  the baseline every kaizen measures against — a living document, not a binder — and why the
  reasons column is what makes a standard teachable and rot-resistant.
- **Grounded and honest:** documents the real current best method (not an aspirational one);
  gets the canon right — the takt/sequence/standard-WIP trio is Toyota's standardized work,
  the steps/key-points/reasons breakdown and four-step training are TWI Job Instruction, and
  the "without a standard there can be no improvement" maxim is flagged as widely attributed
  to Ohno without a firm primary source; invents no statistics; builds in a path to surface
  deviations and better ideas rather than freezing the process.
- **Routes at the seams:** skipped-steps distillation → checklist-design; proposing/aligning
  an improvement → a3-thinking; which task gates the whole effort → theory-of-constraints;
  where the task sits in the end-to-end stream → value-stream-mapping; skill drilling →
  deliberate-practice.
