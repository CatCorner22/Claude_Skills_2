# Evals — writing-skills:adams-plain-grade

## 1. Positive trigger (should load the skill)
> "Rewrite this insurance denial letter for our patients in plain English — someone with low
> reading skills should be able to understand it without help."

Expected: skill loads; the most important point comes first in one simple sentence; sentences
average 10–14 words in active voice; no litigated formulas ("pursuant to", "aforesaid",
doublets) survive the rewrite; any unavoidable technical term (e.g. "deductible") is said
once and explained immediately in plain words; the grade lands at 5th (8th only where
precision demands, stated as such); the meaning of the denial — what was denied, why, what
the patient can do next — is exactly preserved, not softened.

## 2. Near-miss (should NOT load this skill)
> "Draft the internal design doc for the reconciliation engine's new matching pass —
> engineering audience."

Expected: professional/technical internal writing for a jargon-fluent audience — no
plain-grade constraint applies (this is the companion adams-smart-brevity territory, not in
this library). Loading adams-plain-grade here means the description over-triggers on generic
writing asks.

## 2b. Near-miss (precision-drafting guard)
> "Tighten the indemnification clause in this vendor contract — I want it precise and
> enforceable."

Expected: contract-drafting precision work. The Adams/MSCD clarity discipline is relevant
there, but the 5th-grade targets are not — this skill should stay unloaded rather than
push grade-level constraints onto operative legal drafting.

## 3. Quality rubric
- **Does**: main point first; short active sentences; everyday concrete words; one idea per
  sentence; technical terms glossed in place; grade verified 5th (or justified 8th, never
  higher).
- **Teaches**: why litigated language is bad language; why meaning always beats grade (the
  8th-grade fallback exists so precision never excuses complexity); why the read-aloud test
  outranks the readability score.
- **Stays honest**: meaning exactly preserved — no softening, no dropped qualifiers; anything
  that must appear verbatim (regulatory text) is quoted and then explained, not paraphrased.
