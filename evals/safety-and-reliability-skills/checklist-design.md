# Evals — safety-and-reliability-skills:checklist-design

## 1. Positive trigger (should load the skill)
> "Our wire-release SOP is 40 steps, and last month a wire went out without the callback ever being
> done. Design a do-confirm checklist for the moment before we press 'release payments' — killer
> items only."

Expected: skill loads; runs the two-gate killer-item selection over the SOP and returns 5–9 items
*with a rationale per cut*; confirms do-confirm is right for a skilled daily crew and anchors the
card at the "before send" pause point on the release screen; drafts one page in imperative wording;
prescribes a live field-test with the releasing team and a revise cycle; hands ownership to the team
with a named caller, rehearsal, and skip-data review — warning that a mandated card without the
enacted ritual is the Ontario null result.

## 2. Near-miss (bare-"checklist" guard — should NOT load this skill)
> "Run the month-end close checklist for July — walk me through the WD1 subledger tasks and who owns
> each."

Expected: this is *running* an existing checklist, which belongs to the skill that owns the process
(`accounting-skills:month-end-close`). Hard constraint being guarded: the bare word "checklist"
appears throughout this library — many skills ship checklist artifacts — so a bare mention must
never trigger this skill; **design or diagnosis intent is required**. If checklist-design loads
here, tighten the description's trigger phrases.

## 2b. Second near-miss (full-method guard — should NOT load this skill)
> "Every hygienist runs instrument reprocessing differently — document the whole procedure
> end-to-end with sequence, timing, and key points so it's done the same way every time."

Expected: documenting a complete method is `continuous-improvement-skills:standard-work`, not
checklist design — a checklist is a net under the killer steps, not the procedure itself. (A
follow-on ask to distill that SOP's killer items into a read-do card at a pause point *would* be
this skill; co-loading on such a compound ask is correct behavior.)

## 3. Quality rubric
A good response:
- **Does:** selects killer items through both gates (serious harm if missed AND actually skipped in
  practice) with a rationale per cut; matches read-do vs. do-confirm to the crew and task; anchors a
  real pause point; drafts 5–9 imperative one-line items on one page; field-tests and revises; names
  a caller and a skip-data review cadence.
- **Teaches:** explains why checklists fail (too long, no pause point, never trialed, serving
  auditors not the crew) and the paired evidence lesson — WHO 2009 showing the enacted team ritual
  works, Ontario 2014 showing the mandated artifact alone does not; the ritual is the method.
- **Stays honest:** presents the B-17/WHO/Ontario evidence with its [snippet-only] provenance and
  reported-figures caveat; never claims the card alone improves outcomes; keeps the human gate — the
  model drafts the cut, the team challenges it, field-tests, and owns the ritual.
