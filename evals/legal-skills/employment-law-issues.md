# Evals — legal-skills:employment-law-issues

## 1. Positive trigger (should load the skill)
> "We let a salesperson go last Friday for missing targets. A month earlier she'd asked HR whether
> she should be getting paid for the extra hours. Her manager wants to send a release — anything we
> need to worry about?"

Expected: the skill loads. It leads with the exposure — a retaliation claim from the timing between
the wage inquiry and the termination, independent of the targets — then why it matters, what to do
now (preserve records, hold the release, check final-pay timing), the decisions needed, the issues
found with their governing sources (anti-retaliation provisions; exempt classification of inside
sales; the age-claim release rules if she is 40 or over; carve-outs in any confidentiality or
non-disparagement clause), a verify list with sources and check dates, the counsel triggers, and
the ledger. Every number carries its instrument; nothing is stated as current without a date.

## 2. Near-miss A (should NOT load this skill)
> "Can you review my resume for a senior counsel role and suggest how to phrase my experience?"

Expected: this skill does **not** load. Career coaching is out of scope (a career-coaching skill, if
installed, owns it; otherwise plain writing help). *HR* vocabulary alone must not route here.

## 3. Near-miss B (should NOT load this skill)
> "Redline this non-compete so the restricted period and territory are stated clearly."

Expected: `legal-skills:mscd-contract-drafting` loads. The words of a clause, not the issues it
raises. If this skill loads, it should at most hand off after naming the state-law issue.

## 4. Near-miss C (in-domain, tests the verification discipline — should load)
> "How many employees do we need before FMLA applies, and how many weeks of leave is it?"

Expected: the skill loads and answers with the figures marked to their instrument (the FMLA's
coverage and eligibility thresholds and the 12-week entitlement, with the statute named), says
that state family-leave laws add to the federal floor and must be checked for the states involved,
and tells the user to open the live source and date the check. A bare number with no instrument,
or a state figure asserted from memory, is a **failure**.

## 5. Near-miss D (tests the moving-parts discipline — should load)
> "Our handbook's severance template has a broad non-disparagement clause. Is that still OK?"

Expected: the skill loads and explains that the answer depends on an agency position that has moved
and is under reconsideration, names the precedent and its status as last checked with the date,
names the required carve-outs that do not depend on it (agency charges, whistleblower rights), and
routes the current status to a live check. A flat "yes" or "no" is a **failure**.

## 6. Quality rubric
A good response:
- **Exposure first**, in one sentence, then why it matters, then actions by horizon.
- **Every issue has a governing source**, by layer (federal, state, local, contract, practice).
- **Every number has its instrument or a check date**; moving items are dated and routed to the
  live source.
- **Retaliation timing is checked** whenever an adverse action follows protected activity.
- **Counsel triggers are listed**, even when the answer is none.
- **Hands off correctly**: words to the drafting skill, authorities to the research skill.
- **Teaches**: why process is the evidence, and why the thresholds are marked rather than recited.
- **Does not** give state-specific figures from memory, treat a release clause as boilerplate, or
  advise on a termination without the timing check.
