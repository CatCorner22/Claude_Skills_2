# Evals — decision-science-skills:skin-in-the-game

## 1. Positive trigger (should load the skill)
> "Our implementation vendor delivered a system that broke within a month, then quoted
> us extra fees to fix their own defects — they won't stand behind it. We're about to
> negotiate the next contract and I also want our internal acceptance gate fixed:
> right now it's just 'QA passed.' Who signs their name to this, and who eats the loss
> if it fails after go-live?"

Expected: skill loads and runs the symmetry method on both fronts. It maps the risk
transfer (vendor and internal approvers create the risk; the buyer's operation eats
the loss; current binding: none); drafts a graduated symmetry table for the contract —
defect-liability period, milestone withholding and retention, warranty remedies at the
vendor's cost, liability terms for the catastrophic row — and a second one for the
internal gate; converts "QA passed" into named attestations with the five-part anatomy
(name, specific thing, specific means, date, exclusion) plus the decision-maker's
accept-residual-risk attestation; runs the perverse-incentive check (over-caution,
concealment, class-boundary gaming, symmetry theater) and routes the finished rule
set to `coding-agent-skills:rule-stress-testing`; and holds the honesty rail — no
consequence lands on operators who decided nothing. Contract language is offered as
patterns to take to counsel, not as legal advice.

## 2. Near-miss (should NOT load this skill)
> "We're two weeks from launch and the latest test results look bad, but leadership
> says we've come too far to slip the date. Should we still go?"

Expected: `decision-science-skills:the-challenger` owns this — a mid-flight
continue-vs-revise decision under sunk-cost and schedule pressure (burden of proof,
zero-based forward costs, widened options). This skill designs who bears what BEFORE
work begins; it does not adjudicate whether a committed plan should change course. If
it loads here, its description is over-triggering.

## 2b. Near-miss (closer — should NOT load this skill)
> "My leads keep blaming each other and their vendors when projects slip. I want an
> extreme-ownership culture on this team — leaders owning outcomes, no excuses,
> blameless debriefs that still change behavior."

Expected: `coding-agent-skills:extreme-ownership` owns the leadership ethos —
ownership language, the Laws of Combat, debrief culture. "Owning outcomes" sounds
adjacent to consequence design, but the ask is culture and leadership behavior, not
contractual or structural symmetry. The seam: that skill shapes how leaders carry
responsibility; this one writes the tables and attestations that bind whoever chooses
the risk, and it should load only when the user wants that structure designed.

## 3. Quality rubric
A good response:
- **Does the task:** maps the risk transfer with named roles (who decides vs. who
  eats the tail, what currently binds them, what the decider knows that the
  loss-bearer can't see); produces a graduated symmetry table (harm class → who bears
  what → bound how and when), never a flat consequence; binds consequence before the
  work, not after; replaces diffuse approvals with five-part named attestations
  including the exclusion clause; for vendor work, proposes concrete
  defect-liability, warranty, retention, and remedy patterns flagged for counsel
  review; runs the four-question perverse-incentive check and hands the finished rule
  set to `coding-agent-skills:rule-stress-testing`; and applies the power test to
  every row (could the named person have decided otherwise?).
- **Teaches:** explains asymmetry repair — why hidden tail risk is cheap to
  manufacture and why symmetry beats monitoring (the creator's private knowledge
  starts working for safety), crediting the framing to Taleb's Skin in the Game; why
  graduation keeps gates usable (flat-severe seizes them, flat-mild re-frees the
  tail); why a name concentrates what "reviewed by team" diffuses; and why blameless
  system fixes and hard decision-maker symmetry are the same principle at different
  altitudes.
- **Stays honest:** presents Hammurabi §229–233 as the real, graduated stele text and
  the "Roman engineer under the arch" as labeled modern folklore (earliest trace a
  ~2004 USENET signature line) — never cites the legend as history, and keeps the
  [snippet-only] provenance marks; notes §230's bloodline symmetry as a moral
  distance marker rather than sanitizing it; refuses punishment-washing onto the
  powerless even if the user asks for it, offering the system-fix route instead; and
  labels contract language as patterns for counsel, not legal advice.
