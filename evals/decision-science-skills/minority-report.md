# Evals — decision-science-skills:minority-report

## 1. Positive trigger (should load the skill)
> "We're deciding whether to sign the two-year lease or go month-to-month. Run the
> scenarios — I want to see the future outcomes, and what happens if my caseload
> doubles."

Expected: loads the precog cell; frames the decision + horizon + good-outcome line
first; sorts near-certainties from critical uncertainties; builds 3–5 named,
structurally different, Tuesday-concrete futures (not best/expected/worst); files a
full-strength minority report that dissents structurally from the room's lean; runs
the one-variable turn (caseload doubling explicitly — which favored CHOICE it flips,
not which scenario label); flags robust moves that win across futures; reflexivity
line per scenario; probabilities only as base-rate bands or honest ranking; ends
with armed tripwires (observable/watched/thresholded) and the decision log with a
next review trigger.

## 2. Near-miss (adversary-play guard)
> "Set up a war game where you play opposing counsel trying to beat our motion
> strategy move by move."

Expected: `decision-science-skills:tabletop-wargaming` owns adversary play toward
winning. The scenario cell holds futures, including ones with no adversary at all;
if minority-report loads on a red-cell ask, the seam is failing.

## 2b. Near-miss (single-failure guard)
> "Before we commit to this migration, imagine it's six months from now and it
> failed — what killed it?"

Expected: `decision-science-skills:pre-mortem` owns prospective hindsight on ONE
imagined failure before commitment. The cell builds multiple futures where things go
right differently, too. If minority-report loads on a pure pre-mortem ask, tighten
the boundary.

## 3. Quality rubric
- **Does**: decision-framed; certainties/uncertainties sorted with the
  miscategorized-hope test; structurally different named scenarios; minority report
  at full quality (narrative + indicators + sensitivity, presented by name); the
  one-variable turn table with flips identified; robust moves listed; reflexivity
  line per scenario; honest probability treatment; tripwires with watcher and
  threshold; decision log closed with next trigger.
- **Teaches**: why structure beats amplitude (best/expected/worst is one future at
  three volumes); the Shell lineage and why unsurprisability, not prediction, is the
  product; Merton's self-fulfilling/self-defeating prophecies as the reflexivity
  anchor; the parliamentary origin of "minority report."
- **Stays honest**: the dissenting scenario never straw-manned or merged away; no
  decorative percentages; the precrime moral held (forecasts inform choices — no
  person or project is convicted on an unhappened future); fiction cited as labeled
  homage, mechanism cited as documented practice; the human owns the decision.
