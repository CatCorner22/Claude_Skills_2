# Evals — decision-science-skills:ulysses-pact

## 1. Positive trigger (should load the skill)
> "We broke our own rule again — third time someone's force-pushed a shared branch at
> 2am during an incident and burned the next morning cleaning it up. Stop us from
> overriding this under pressure. Bind my future self."

Expected: skill loads; runs the degradation-scenario interview in the current calm
state (record → pressure class → tell → typical bad move → bill); drafts one narrow
pact per rule using the full template (rule, trigger context, second signature from a
real refuser, cooling-off, pre-committed default, unbinding criteria, review date);
insists on pre-decided unbinding criteria and explains the straitjacket failure mode;
registers the pact in the path of the act (contributing doc, runbook, assistant's pacts
file) with the adoption date and a why paragraph in the user's own words; sets the
review date; explains that future enforcement will be quotation of the user's own
words, never scolding, and that mid-crisis renegotiation is refused by construction
with the objection logged.

## 2. Near-miss (commitment-revision guard)
> "Evidence has changed mid-project and leadership keeps saying we've come too far to
> slip the date. Should we still be go, or does this commitment deserve revisiting?"

Expected: `decision-science-skills:the-challenger` owns the revision decision on an
existing commitment when evidence changes — zero-basing, burden of proof, sunk costs
excluded. Ulysses-pact *creates* binding commitments against a foreseen worse self; it
does not adjudicate whether a live plan continues. The seam is a partnership (a good
pact names the evidence that routes you to the-challenger), but if ulysses-pact loads
on a continue-vs-revise ask, the boundary is failing.

## 2b. Near-miss (routine-automation guard)
> "I keep forgetting to write up my rep log after each practice session — help me make
> it automatic so it just happens."

Expected: `learning-skills:habit-design` owns making a wanted routine automatic (cue →
routine → reward, implementation intentions). Ulysses-pact bars an *unwanted* act under
pressures where routines fail. If ulysses-pact loads on a make-it-automatic ask,
tighten the boundary.

## 3. Quality rubric
- **Does**: interview run in calm state with tells that are observable from outside the
  feeling; every template field filled in the user's own words; unbinding criteria
  about the world changing, never about the pressure being strong; pact registered in
  the path of the act, not in a drawer; enforcement demonstrated as verbatim quotation
  of rule + why paragraph + exception machinery, then stopping; near-misses logged for
  the review date.
- **Teaches**: why degradation is predictable in kind though not in timing (the calm
  self's judgment vs the pressured self's presence); why unbinding criteria separate a
  pact from a straitjacket and who owns the exit; why cooling-off works by outlasting
  the degradation peak rather than forbidding the act; why quotation persuades where
  scolding gets routed around; override (through the pact's machinery) vs unbinding
  (evidence-based release) as distinct doors.
- **Stays honest**: clinical provenance held with its marks (psychiatric advance
  directives as Ulysses contracts [peer-reviewed]) and no invented statistics; the
  human authors every rule and owns every override — the assistant never invents pact
  terms or refuses beyond the pact's own text; institutional dual-approval named in
  prose as the same mechanism without pretending this skill implements payment
  controls; sensitive pact content steered to the git-ignored private file.
