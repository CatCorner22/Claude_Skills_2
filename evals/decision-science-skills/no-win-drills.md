# Evals — decision-science-skills:no-win-drills

## 1. Positive trigger (should load the skill)
> "We're six weeks from a regulatory filing date and we can't hit it with full scope at
> our quality bar — every option is bad. Run us through a kobayashi maru on this so the
> team practices making the least-worst call before we have to make it for real."

Expected: skill loads; the LLM designs the drill itself (door inventory, diegetic
closures, clean-exit self-attack) rather than letting the team author it; declares the
no-win contract before play; runs the escalation beats including the tempting door and
the announced futility point; demands a declared save order before the terminal
decision; if anyone quietly redefines success mid-drill, stops play and forces the
physics-vs-policy declaration; debriefs in three passes grading the four process
dimensions and states aloud that a high process grade with a total-loss outcome is the
drill working; offers the constraint audit (physics vs policy, transparency test) for
the real decision the drill rehearses.

## 2. Near-miss (adversary-play guard)
> "Set up an exercise where a red cell plays a ransomware crew against our
> incident-response plan — injects, adjudication, the works — and we try to beat them."

Expected: `decision-science-skills:tabletop-wargaming` owns multi-party adversarial
exercises played toward *winning*, with roles, injects, and adjudication. No-win drills
remove the winning move by construction and grade only process. If no-win-drills loads
on an adversary-play ask, the seam is failing.

## 2b. Near-miss (pre-commitment guard)
> "Before we lock in this migration plan, assume it's a year from now and the whole
> thing failed. What sank it?"

Expected: `decision-science-skills:pre-mortem` owns prospective hindsight on a plan
before commitment — imagining failure *causes*. No-win drills train choosing among
only-bad options inside an unwinnable situation; they do not generate failure reasons
for a plan that might still succeed. If no-win-drills loads here, tighten the boundary.

## 3. Quality rubric
- **Does**: drill designed by the LLM with every exit inventoried and closed
  diegetically, then attacked by its own designer before play; the no-win contract
  declared to players up front; escalation follows the beat script with consequences
  for every decision; a save order demanded before the terminal choice; the debrief
  runs facts → process grades → emotional debrief, grading loss-minimization, save
  order, communication under futility, and frame honesty — never the outcome.
- **Teaches**: why the winning move's presence contaminates the lesson (the
  emergency-medicine patient-death finding, held with its provenance mark); decision
  quality vs outcome quality as different variables; the physics-vs-policy audit and
  the three-part transparency test (declared, policy-not-physics, owned consequences);
  why the quiet Kirk move is goalpost-moving, not simulator-hacking.
- **Stays honest**: the Kirk blade taught as the classroom version (Conti & Caroland —
  assigned, declared, graded), never as "cheat and be rewarded"; hidden reframes graded
  as concealment even when clever; no invented statistics beyond the dossier's
  documented findings; fiction names stay in triggers and homage note, never presented
  as the source of authority — the documented practices are; the LLM generates and
  escalates but the humans decide and own the debrief conclusions.
