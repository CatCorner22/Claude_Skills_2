# Evals — collaboration-skills:meeting-design

## 1. Positive trigger (should load the skill)
> "Our weekly team sync has grown to 12 people and 90 minutes, and nothing ever
> actually gets decided — we just go around the room. Can you fix the agenda? Here's
> what we currently cover: project updates, blockers, upcoming deadlines, and any
> other business."

Expected: skill loads; runs the should-this-meeting-exist test first (updates and
deadlines flow async — drafts the status post rather than apologizing for suggesting
it); rewrites the surviving items as decision questions, each with a timebox and a
named decision rule (single owner / consent / consult-then-decide / vote) declared
before discussion opens; proposes a pre-read with silent reading time at the start,
attributed as documented Amazon practice; adds the visible parking lot, the
owner-and-date rule for actions, and the closing read-back published as a decision
log; flags the recurring meeting for a standing audit with an honest
person-hours cost (no invented dollar statistics).

## 2. Near-miss (workshop-facilitation guard)
> "We're planning a two-day improvement workshop where the intake team redesigns
> their own process at the gemba — help me facilitate it."

Expected: `continuous-improvement-skills:kaizen-and-codesign` owns kaizen events and
co-design workshop facilitation. meeting-design covers the ordinary working meeting —
decision agendas, pre-reads, decision logs. If meeting-design loads on a kaizen/
workshop ask, the boundary is failing.

## 2b. Near-miss (time-pressure escalation guard)
> "A payment looks wrong and my manager approved it — I need to escalate this to the
> treasurer in the next ten minutes. How do I structure what I say?"

Expected: `safety-and-reliability-skills:sbar-structured-communication` owns
escalation and transfer-of-work communication under time pressure (SBAR, read-back,
graded assertiveness). meeting-design designs deliberate decision forums, not urgent
upward communication. If it loads here, tighten the seam.

## 3. Quality rubric
- **Does**: applies the existence test before designing anything (and drafts the
  async post when the answer is "this is an email"); every agenda item phrased as an
  answerable decision question with timebox, decision rule, and rule-holder named
  up front; silent-start pre-read mechanics specified; actions carry owner + date;
  close is a read-back published as a decision log with `REVISIT IF` triggers;
  recurring meetings get the audit protocol.
- **Teaches**: why topic-agendas produce discussion and decision-agendas produce
  outcomes (a topic has no finish line); why naming the decision rule up front
  prevents fake consensus (the unspoken everyone-must-agree default); why silent
  reading beats presenting (pre-reads go unread; prose is a stricter argument than
  slides); why a recurring meeting is a standing cost that renews by default.
- **Stays honest**: silent-start attributed as documented Amazon practice, not
  oversold; no invented meeting-cost statistics (person-hours arithmetic only); the
  human ratifies decision rules and makes the calls — the assistant drafts agendas
  and logs, never decides; killing a meeting is presented as a method outcome, not
  performative minimalism.
