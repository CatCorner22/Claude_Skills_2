# Evals — collaboration-skills:stakeholder-mapping

## 1. Positive trigger (should load the skill)
> "We're migrating intake to a new routed-queue system. The claims team lead openly
> hates it, finance is lukewarm, legal hasn't even been told, and I don't have
> authority over two of the teams that have to change how they work. Map the
> stakeholders — who can sink this, who can save it, and what do I do about each?"

Expected: skill loads; builds the candidate list beyond the org chart (approvers,
behavior-changers, status-quo beneficiaries, inheritors); places each on the
power-interest grid with evidence-vs-inference tags and a date; flags "legal untold" as
a high-power ambush risk; assigns quadrant moves with the folk-simplification caution
(defaults, not personality types); writes the RACI so consulted means before the
decision; builds a Cohen & Bradford currencies plan for the teams the user cannot
compel; offers stakeholder-shaped pre-mortem narratives; sets re-map triggers at
milestones; keeps real names out of committed files (roles in the map, names in the
git-ignored private file).

## 2. Near-miss (stakeholder-interview guard)
> "I've got a stakeholder interview tomorrow with the process owner — she knows how the
> real workflow runs but she gives short, guarded answers. Help me plan the questions."

Expected: `collaboration-skills:disarming-elicitation` owns the stakeholder interview —
the stance and question sequence that gets a willing expert talking. stakeholder-mapping
maps *who matters*; it does not run the conversation. If stakeholder-mapping loads on an
interview-prep ask, the boundary is failing.

## 2b. Near-miss (negotiation guard)
> "The vendor came back with an 18% renewal increase and I have to push back next week —
> help me prepare for the negotiation."

Expected: `decision-science-skills:principled-negotiation` owns bargaining with a
counterpart — interests, BATNA, objective criteria, rehearsal. A named counterpart
across the table is past mapping; stakeholder-mapping's own body hands this off. If it
loads here, tighten the seam.

## 3. Quality rubric
- **Does**: candidate list surfaces non-obvious stakeholders (veto-holders, status-quo
  beneficiaries, inheritors); grid placements rated relative to *this* work, each
  tagged evidence or inference, the map dated; quadrant moves assigned with instruments
  wired (meeting-design for forums, disarming-elicitation for conversations,
  feedback-that-lands for behavior, principled-negotiation for counterparts); RACI with
  exactly one A per row and C's consulted before decisions; currencies plan names the
  ask, the diagnosis, and offers the user can genuinely keep, traded openly; re-map
  triggers set at milestones, reorgs, and contradictions, with deltas recorded.
- **Teaches**: why mapping early is cheap and mapping late is forensics (surprise
  converts neutrals into procedural opponents); why the grid allocates scarce
  engagement effort and why placements decay (stakes shift, so the map is a snapshot);
  why "consulted after the decision" is the RACI failure that matters; why influence
  without authority is open exchange and a hidden ledger is politics.
- **Stays honest**: the power-interest grid cited to Johnson & Scholes (1999) / Eden &
  Ackermann (1998) with the open correction that Mendelow's 1981 paper carries a
  power/dynamism matrix — the finding sourced to that paper's own published abstract
  (AIS eLibrary, ICIS 1981 paper 20), with the full paper text stated as unread;
  Freeman (1984) taught as the theory's anchor, never as coiner of
  "stakeholder" (SRI ~1963, credited by Freeman himself); RACI's no-inventor status
  stated rather than a vendor origin story repeated; quadrant labels flagged as
  folk-simplifications layered on the grid; no invented adoption or success statistics;
  never commits a real power map — roles in git, names in the private file.
