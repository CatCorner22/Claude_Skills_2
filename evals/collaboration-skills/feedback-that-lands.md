# Evals — collaboration-skills:feedback-that-lands

## 1. Positive trigger (should load the skill)
> "I'm at my limit with a teammate — he talks over people in every meeting, his
> numbers are half-checked, and when I hinted at it last month he got defensive and
> sulked for a week. I need to say something real this time without torching the
> relationship. Help me figure out what to say."

Expected: skill loads; narrows the vent to ONE topic for this conversation (and says
why — a list is an indictment); drafts an SBI script attributed to the Center for
Creative Leadership's model — one specific situation, camera-test behavior (no
"dismissive", no motive-reading), impact owned as the speaker's experience; frames
the ask forward as Goldsmith-style feedforward ("next time, would you…"); calibrates
privacy and timing; offers to rehearse playing the receiver, including the defensive
version, and prepares the giver for the receiver's likely trigger (Stone & Heen's
truth / relationship / identity) so the defensiveness is anticipated, not just
survived.

## 2. Near-miss (work-product critique guard)
> "Here's the migration plan I wrote. Tear it apart — be honest about whether it's
> any good before I send it up."

Expected: `coding-agent-skills:sparring-partner` owns critique of a submitted work
product (verdict, strengths, sparring feedback, action plan). feedback-that-lands
addresses a person's behavior in a working relationship, not the quality of an
artifact. If it loads on a "critique my document" ask, the boundary is failing.

## 2b. Near-miss (review-process guard)
> "How should we structure PR approvals on this team — who reviews, when to rebase
> versus merge, and how small should changes be?"

Expected: `coding-agent-skills:git-and-code-review` owns code-review mechanics and
process. feedback-that-lands upgrades the TONE of a review comment (character-language
to behavior-language), not the review workflow. If it loads on a process-design ask,
tighten the seam.

## 3. Quality rubric
- **Does**: one topic per conversation, enforced; SBI elements each pass their test
  (specificity / camera / ownership); the request is feedforward, not relitigation;
  privacy and timing addressed; praise, when in scope, gets the same
  situation-behavior-evidence specificity; rehearsal offered with the assistant as
  receiver; on receiving-side asks, runs the protocol — separate data from delivery,
  name the firing trigger, ask for the behavior behind the label, close the loop.
- **Teaches**: the fundamental attribution error named plainly as why
  character-language triggers defense and behavior-language doesn't; why
  impact-as-my-experience is undeniable while impact-as-accusation is debatable; why
  feedback hoarded for the formal review arrives too late to use; that the receiver
  controls whether feedback becomes change, so receiving is taught with equal weight.
- **Stays honest**: frameworks attributed correctly (SBI — Center for Creative
  Leadership; feedforward — Marshall Goldsmith; truth/relationship/identity triggers —
  Stone & Heen, *Thanks for the Feedback*) with no invented statistics; the assistant
  drafts and rehearses but the human judges the relationship and has the conversation;
  the vent's character-verdicts are stripped from the script, not laundered into
  politer character-verdicts; receiving guidance never becomes "all feedback is
  valid" — the receiver may decline, on the data, after extracting it.
