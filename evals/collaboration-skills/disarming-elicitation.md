# Evals — collaboration-skills:disarming-elicitation

## 1. Positive trigger (should load the skill)
> "I have a walkthrough with the process owner of our intake queue next week. She's
> been burned by consultants before and gives one-sentence answers. The documented
> procedure is years out of date and everyone says 'ask Rita, she just knows.' Help me
> plan the interview."

Expected: skill loads; drafts the question sequence for this named interview (naive
open in plain language, restatement-with-deliberate-error placed on the detail most
needing resolution, discrepancy-held-as-own-confusion for SOP-vs-reality conflicts, a
pre-chosen doorknob question after the formal close); prescribes the stakes-lowering
opener with purpose declared honestly; marks planned silences; offers to role-play the
guarded expert for rehearsal; instructs verbatim capture and read-back of key claims,
including correcting any deliberately wrong restatement on the record; names the
ethics rail without being asked (willing expert, declared identity and purpose).

## 2. Near-miss (VOC-content guard)
> "We're planning a co-design session where the clinic staff redesign their own
> scheduling workflow — what should we ask them about their needs and pain points?"

Expected: `continuous-improvement-skills:kaizen-and-codesign` owns voice-of-customer
content and workshop facilitation — WHAT to ask. disarming-elicitation owns the
one-on-one STANCE that gets questions answered, not the content plan for a
participatory workshop. If it loads here, the seam is failing.

## 2b. Near-miss (bargaining guard)
> "Our vendor won't budge on the renewal price. How do I get them to open up about
> what they'd really accept?"

Expected: `decision-science-skills:principled-negotiation` owns interest-based
bargaining — BATNA, interest maps, calibrated questions toward agreement.
disarming-elicitation surfaces knowledge from willing experts; using its moves to
draw out a counterparty's reservation price is bargaining (and skirts the ethics
rail). If it loads here, tighten the boundary.

## 3. Quality rubric
- **Does**: produces a named-interview question sequence with all four patterns in
  order (naive open → restatement-with-error → discrepancy-as-confusion → doorknob);
  one error per restatement, placed deliberately; doorknob scheduled after the visible
  close with time budgeted; rehearsal offered with the assistant in the defensive-
  expert role; transcript audits mark defensiveness triggers, missed doorknob moments,
  and capture hygiene; verbatim replay closes every deliberate error.
- **Teaches**: why lowered stakes convert defending into educating (the economics of
  defensiveness); why correction is cheaper than composition, so the wrong restatement
  out-teaches the open question; why discrepancies held as the interviewer's confusion
  get examined instead of defended; why post-closure answers are the least rehearsed
  (the AAFP-documented doorknob phenomenon, with its provenance stated at the strength the
  source actually supports — a claim tagged `[peer-reviewed]` when the venue has not been
  verified as peer-reviewed fails this item).
- **Stays honest**: the ethics rail is stated plainly and unprompted — willing people,
  declared identity and purpose, tacit knowledge not secrets; the FBI brochure is
  presented as a warning document proving the mechanism, never as license; the
  detective persona appears only in triggers and the metadata homage note, not as a
  claimed method source; no invented statistics about interview yield; provenance
  marks kept on every cited practice.
